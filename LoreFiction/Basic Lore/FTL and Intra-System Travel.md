FTL and In-System Travel

Corridors are the only means of faster-than-light (FTL) travel. They allow ships to move between star systems, but they never exit directly at a colony. Corridors open wherever their current anchor position happens to be in the target system which is often far from inhabited locations.

Once a ship exits the corridor, it must travel to its destination (colony, outpost, station) using sub-light engines. These can handle astronomical distances efficiently, but not instantly.

Shorter intra-system travel (e.g. from a gate to a nearby orbital station) may take hours to a day. Longer treks across the system, say from the gate to a colony several AU away, can take several days to a week, depending on ship specs and fuel reserves.

This sub-light travel is relatively safe. Aside from basic risks like equipment failure, fuel mismanagement, or navigation errors, most corridor-related threats like Corridor Radiation and Static Fog do not persist outside of the corridor itself. Once in normal space, crews only contend with standard spacefaring hazards.

Unlike Corridor Radiation or Static Fog, Vacuum Bloom remains a wildcard in intra-system travel. Since it drifts and latches to heat and metal, it may be present in systems with recent ship traffic or poor decontamination enforcement, but it’s not exclusive to corridor zones. It can spread in normal space if introduced by contaminated ships; including spreading to inhabited areas such as colonies, space stations or outposts.

Outposts and stations are common waypoints between gates and settlements, especially in sprawling systems with distant colonies. These midpoints serve as additional:

- Refueling stops

- Emergency repair docks

- Relay points for physical data or small-scale cargo

- Rest locations

Travel times and system layout matter. Even if a colony is “connected” via corridor, actual arrival depends on local infrastructure, fuel, crew skill, and distance. There is no “direct drop-off.”
