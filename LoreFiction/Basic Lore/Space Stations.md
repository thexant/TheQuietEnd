Space stations serve as larger, more developed variants of outposts or smaller Civilian settlements. Unlike colonies, they typically do not sustain large civilian populations and instead generally house at most 25-75 individuals, plus the transient presence of visiting ship crews. Those who live aboard stations may have varying roles: some work as mechanics at nearby Gates, others at might work at nearby military installations, research platforms, or regional relay points for logistics and communication. Usually only a small group of those living on a Space Station have jobs aboard the space station; most work off-station in nearby local space, returning only after the end of their shifts.

Ownership is usually centralized, with the core government overseeing most stations, though some may be administered by local colonies depending on their region and purpose. Their locations are strategically chosen, to act as critical “first stops” for arriving vessels or be for easy access to multiple different locations nearby.

Space stations are not self-sufficient. They rely heavily on external supply runs and consistent maintenance to remain operational, though with proper care they are built to endure for decades. Accessibility ranges from open ports to heavily restricted facilities depending on the station’s purpose. While life aboard a station is utilitarian and often monotonous, crews may include a mix of maintenance personnel, engineers, medical staff, shopkeepers, scientists, and security, reflecting the station’s specific needs.
