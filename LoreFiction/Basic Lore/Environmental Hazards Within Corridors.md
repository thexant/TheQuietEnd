Even when a Corridor is stable and a Gate is fully operational, the internal medium of the Corridor is an actively hostile environment. The following subsections detail the persistent, unavoidable environmental threats that all crews must be prepared to mitigate during every transit. These are not risks that can be avoided, only managed.

### Pervasive Threat: Corridor Radiation

Corridor Radiation is composed of exotic particulate matter that is invariably present in all active Corridors. It is an aggressive agent that degrades technology and biological tissue, making effective shielding and decontamination procedures absolutely essential for survival.

**Effects of Exposure**

• **On Ship Systems:** The radiation has a corrosive effect on exposed hull materials and causes significant electrical instability, particularly impacting sensitive navigation and power systems. Over time, it also degrades optical sensors.

• **On Human Biology:** For unshielded personnel, exposure is agonizing and rapidly fatal. Initial symptoms, including intense surface burns and disorientation, appear within hours. This progresses to acute radiation syndrome, systemic organ failure, tissue necrosis, and ultimately death.

The primary method for managing this threat is the use of robust ship shielding during transit and mandatory passage through the full decontamination facilities present at all gated exits. Ships forced to use ungated exit points face a significant risk of lethal contamination.

### Sporadic Threat: Static Fog

Static Fog is a rare but severe electro-magnetic storm system that can form within Corridors or linger near their exit points. Its primary danger is the systematic and silent disabling of critical ship functions, creating a state of cascading failure.

• **Minor Encounters:** A vessel passing through a mild Fog will experience disruption of sensors, communications, and navigation systems, requiring manual intervention from the crew.

• **Severe Encounters:** A severe Fog can be catastrophic, powerful enough to fry a ship's core power systems, disable life support entirely, and even cause hull ruptures through extreme magnetic shearing forces.

Unlike Corridor Radiation, Static Fog does not directly harm biological tissue. Instead, it creates a silent, system-wide failure state that is equally lethal, leaving a crew to face depressurization, power loss, and navigational blindness in an already hazardous environment.

These internal hazards, both pervasive and sporadic, demonstrate that survival is not guaranteed even on a successful transit, leading to ancillary threats that can follow a vulnerable ship into normal space.

## Other Threats of Interstellar Travel

The dangers of Corridor travel do not end upon exiting the transit medium. The network itself, by its very nature, creates vulnerabilities that foster both biological and human threats. These hazards can spread between systems via contaminated ships or prey upon the isolation and weakness of travelers in the vast distances between secure ports.

### Biological Contamination: Vacuum Bloom

Vacuum Bloom is a non-sentient, fungus-like organism that drifts through deep space as a nearly undetectable cloud of spores. It is an indifferent hazard, posing no intelligent threat, but its biological imperatives make it a dire risk to all human infrastructure. The organism passively seeks heat and metal, causing it to latch onto ships, stations, and colonies.

• **On Structures:** Once attached, the Bloom spreads like a voracious rot, consuming infrastructure from within. If an infestation is not contained immediately, it can render an entire vessel or habitat a total loss.

• **On Humans:** The inhalation of Vacuum Bloom spores is fatal. The organism grows within the lungs, causing rapid respiratory failure and toxic shock, after which it consumes the victim's body.

The spread of Vacuum Bloom is a direct consequence of ship traffic moving between systems, an ever-present biological risk carried on the hulls of the unwary.

### Human Predation: Pirates and Bandits

The decay of interstellar infrastructure, fading governmental oversight, and the profound isolation of many colonies and trade routes create ideal conditions for piracy. These groups range from single desperate crews to loosely organized factions. Their vessels are a direct reflection of their opportunistic nature: a patchwork of repurposed industrial freighters, stolen civilian craft, and gutted mining rigs retrofitted with weapon mounts.

Their methods and targets are dictated by pragmatism. They prey on the weak: isolated vessels, remote outposts with minimal defenses, and colonies struggling with severed supply lines. They rarely attack well-defended, operational Gates, as these structures are as vital to their own mobility as they are to legitimate commerce. Their existence is a constant, unpredictable human threat in the gaps of a failing system.

From immediate predators to slower biological decay, these ancillary threats pave the way for the ultimate, systemic hazard created by the Corridor network: isolation.
