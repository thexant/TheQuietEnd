# Bandits, Pirates, and Raiders

The gradual decay of widespread authority and the increasing instability of the Corridor Network have created a pervasive power vacuum across the colonized galaxy. Within these lawless margins, various predatory groups collectively referred to as bandits, pirates, and raiders thrive by exploiting the isolation of individual systems and the inherent dangers of interstellar travel.

## I. Nature and Status

These various groups do not constitute an official "faction," political body, or unified ideology. Instead, they make up a broad category of criminal activity driven by opportunistic survival and profit.

- **Hidden Presence**: These entities exist in countless hidden pockets of the galaxy, deliberately operating outside the bounds of any authoritative oversight or legal jurisdiction.

- **Decentralized Elements**: They are composed of desperate crews in repurposed ships, often striking out from the margins of society without any central coordination.

- **Pragmatic Criminality**: Their motivations are simple: the acquisition of resources, fuel, and cargo through the exploitation of systemic decay.


## II. Operational Areas

Raiders focus their efforts on star systems and transit zones where organized protection is at its weakest.

- **Unpopulated Systems:** They commonly lurk in star systems characterized by minimal or abandoned habitation, utilizing decommissioned outposts as staging grounds.

- **Transit Ambush Points:** Specialized traps are frequently established near unmanned Corridor Gates or deep within local space along established travel routes.

- **Strategic Positioning:** They specifically target locations where the response time for organized aid is guaranteed to be critically slow or nonexistent.

## III. Tactical Targets and Constraints

The activities of these lawless groups are shaped by the realities of space and the limitations of technology.

- **Vulnerable Assets**: Typical targets include lone vessels in transit, remote outposts with minimal defenses, or colonies that have been physically cut off from the wider network by a Corridor shift.

- **Infrastructure Preservation**: As a pragmatic constraint, functioning Corridor Gates are rarely targeted for destruction. Attacking a Gate disrupts a major trade artery essential for everyone’s survival, including the raiders' own.

- **Exploitation of Information Lag**: Raiders take advantage of the fact that all information must be travel physically. This delay ensures that by the time word of an attack reaches a defensive force, the perpetrators have typically escaped.
