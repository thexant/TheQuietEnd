# Independent Systems

Independent Systems are settlements or star systems that attempt to manage themselves. Rather than aligning with a larger ideological bloc, these systems prioritize sovereign self-governance and operational autonomy.

## I. Criteria for Independence

Independence is not merely a political declaration but a status earned through logistical redundancy and infrastructure. To maintain this status, a system generally will have more than a single colony, station or outpost.

- **System Redundancy**: Independent Systems typically possess multiple established locations, such as a cluster of planetary colonies, an integrated space station, or a network of industrial outposts.

- **Resource Management**: This internal complexity allows for a higher degree of self-sustainability, ensuring that the failure of one installation does not result in the collapse of the entire system.

- **Operational Autonomy**: These systems manage their own local laws, defense militias, and resource distribution without seeking significant oversight from external groups or authorities.


## II. Confidence in Resilience

A defining characteristic of an Independent System is its psychological and physical preparation for isolation. While every human settlement is fundamentally dependent on the interstellar Corridor Network for long-term viability, these systems operate with a high degree of confidence in their own endurance.

- **Sustained Survival**: Independent Systems are often prepared to sustain themselves for extended periods if their connection to the wider galaxy is severed.

- **Isolation Preparedness**: They operate on the principle that if a Corridor collapses, external help regardless of alliance status is unlikely to arrive in time, or at all.

- **Hopeful Reconnection**: Their strategy is built on outlasting periods of total isolation until a Corridor shift or a successful Gate repositioning allows for reconnection to the interstellar network.


## III. Strategic Neutrality and Shifting Alignments

Independent Systems are not necessarily isolationist; they are pragmatic. They do not maintain a permanent fealty to any single power, instead interacting with various other factions as necessity dictates.

- **Fluid Partnerships**: An Independent System may trade with the **Autonomous Syndicates**, hire **Transit Security Contractors**, or maintain professional diplomatic ties with **True Allegiance** systems without officially joining their ranks.

- **Movement Toward Groups**: As the Corridor Network has become increasingly unstable, many systems that once prided themselves on total independence have strategically moved toward loosely organized groups, such as the Syndicates, to pool resources and mitigate risk.

- **Holdouts**: Despite these pressures, a segment of systems remains steadfastly independent. These holdouts believe that pooling resources with others creates a chain of dependency that is just as fragile as the centralized systems that failed in the past.
